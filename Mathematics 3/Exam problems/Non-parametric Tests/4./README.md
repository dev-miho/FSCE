## Problem Statement
I
n a popular video game, to obtain a new hero, players need to open "mystery packages".
When opening the packages, the new heroes can be **common, strong, epic, and legendary**.
The game developers claim that the new heroes in the packages are distributed in a ratio of **10:6:3:1**, respectively.

A passionate player was skeptical of the developers' claim and bought **500 such packages**.
He opened all of them and got the following results:

## Observed Data

| Hero Type      | Common | Strong | Epic | Legendary |
|----------------|--------|--------|------|-----------|
| Number of Heroes | 270  | 140    | 65   | 25        |

## Question

> With a significance level of **5%**, can the player claim that the distribution
> of heroes in the packages is the same as the distribution of heroes
> according to the game developers?
